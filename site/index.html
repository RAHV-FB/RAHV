Buying here works differently.

Representation

Don't assume the person showing you a property is representing you the way a buyer's agent might in the U.S. It's worth understanding who everyone in the transaction is working for and how they are paid.

The arras agreement

An arras agreement can come relatively early in the process and can carry real financial consequences. It isn't something to treat as a casual reservation form, and the wording should be reviewed before a substantial deposit is transferred.

The building matters

With an older Barcelona apartment, the property itself is only part of the picture. The building, lift, façade, roof, community accounts and planned works can all matter.

Residency is separate

Buying a property and obtaining the right to live in Spain are separate questions. The previous property-based Golden Visa route is no longer available for new purchases.

Where should you look?

A €1M–€3M search can look very different depending on whether you want a central apartment, a quieter residential area, a house with a garden or the coast.

Barcelona city

Central Barcelona is mainly an apartment market: Eixample, Sarrià–Sant Gervasi, Les Corts and Pedralbes, Gràcia, Poblenou. At this level, floor, light, terrace, parking, renovation and the condition of the building can matter as much as the neighbourhood name.

Metropolitan Barcelona

If you say “Barcelona” but describe a detached house, a garden, a garage and quieter streets, it may make sense to look beyond the city centre, in places such as Sant Just Desvern, Esplugues or Sant Cugat.

Sitges & Castelldefels

Useful to consider if the coast, outdoor space and larger homes matter while keeping a strong connection to Barcelona. There are apartments and villas from the beachfront up into the hills, and parking and transport work differently town by town.

Costa Brava

A different search again. Town, access, terrain, sea view, outdoor space and how often you'll use the property matter more than a single regional €/m² figure. Begur, Llafranc and S'Agaró are typical starting points.

A few questions worth asking early

  • Who is actually representing me in the transaction?
  • What should I check before signing arras?
  • What should I know about the building as well as the apartment?
  • Should I be looking in Barcelona city or outside it?
  • If I need financing, when should I start?
  • What should I budget beyond the property price?

The answers depend on the buyer, the property, the financing and the timing, which is why they're worth discussing early.

Questions people ask

Can an American buy property in Barcelona?

Yes. You don't need to be a Spanish resident to own property here. Residency and the purchase itself are separate matters.

Does buying property give me Spanish residency?

No. The property-based Golden Visa route has ended for new purchases. If you plan to live in Spain, immigration status needs to be considered separately.

Do I need an NIE?

You will normally need an NIE, the Spanish identification number for foreigners, for the transaction. Serious buyers should deal with it early rather than leaving it until the final stages.

What is arras?

A private agreement commonly signed before completion, usually with a substantial deposit. The terms carry real consequences once signed, so the wording should be reviewed before money is transferred.

Can I get a mortgage if I live in the U.S.?

Non-resident financing is available, but lenders generally expect substantial buyer equity and will need financial and source-of-funds documentation. The exact terms depend on the buyer and the lender.

Can I start looking before I come to Barcelona?

Yes. The initial search can be narrowed remotely, and the local team can arrange viewings, including video where useful.

Where should I look with a €1M–€3M budget?

It depends on what you're after: a central apartment, a larger family property, a house with outdoor space or the coast. That's usually one of the first things worth discussing on a call.

Should I have my own lawyer?

Independent legal review of the property, the contract and the transaction is important. Steve can introduce appropriate professionals where needed.
