Steve Howard

I've lived in Barcelona since 2006. Before moving to Spain, I worked in real estate in California and held real-estate licences in California and Florida.

How I ended up here

I moved to Barcelona in 2006 because of my relationship with a Spanish woman. There was no larger plan behind it. We built a life here, I raised my family here, and at some point Barcelona stopped being the place I had moved to and simply became home.

Twenty years in a city teaches you how it actually works: which streets are quiet and which only look quiet on a Sunday, how neighbourhoods change block by block, what it's like to deal with a building community, a renovation or a school run here. Most of what I know about Barcelona comes from living in it, and that turns out to be the useful part when someone is deciding where to buy.

Before Barcelona

Before Spain, I worked in real estate in California, in and around Beverly Hills, and held real-estate licences in California and Florida. Those licences are no longer active, and I'm not a licensed estate agent in Catalonia. The formal property work here, meaning the search, the viewings and the transaction, is handled by the licensed local team I work with.

What stayed with me from those years is a working knowledge of how buying property works in the United States: who represents the buyer, how offers move forward, what closing looks like, what an agent is expected to do. American buyers arrive with that system in their heads, and much of my work is explaining where the process here matches those expectations and where it doesn't.

Living here

A lot of the early conversations are about matching what people say with what they actually want.

Someone may tell me they want to live in Barcelona and then describe a detached house, a garden, quiet streets and easy parking. That usually means we should at least compare the city with places such as Sant Just Desvern or Sant Cugat before anyone books viewings.

With an Eixample apartment, the renovation inside is only part of the question. The building, the light, the lift, the noise and any planned community works can change the picture, and two apartments on the same block can be very different once you look at those things.

Some buyers end up happier in Sitges, in Castelldefels or in a town on the Costa Brava. Working that out early saves wasted trips and wasted weekends of viewings.

How I work

It starts with a call. You tell me your budget, your timing and what you're looking for, and I'll tell you honestly what that points to and what I'd ask next. You don't need to know the neighbourhoods before we speak; that's part of the conversation. Some people have a specific brief, others just know they want to spend more time here and are starting to think seriously about buying.

When the search gets serious, the licensed local team takes over the property side: the formal search, the viewings and the transaction. They can search across a wider agency network and deal directly with other agents where needed, so the search follows what you're looking for rather than one office's listings. I stay involved as the person you already know and can call.

If you're still in the U.S., a lot can happen before you get on a plane. Areas and properties can be narrowed down from a distance, and the local team can arrange viewings, including by video where it makes sense. When a question is legal, tax, mortgage or technical, it goes to the professional who handles that work.

Local contacts

Finding the property is only part of moving into it. After twenty years here I know people: lawyers, mortgage professionals, architects and builders, and the practical contacts for utilities and paperwork after a purchase. Where a good introduction exists, I'll make it. I don't replace those professionals, and I don't give legal, tax, mortgage or financial advice myself.

If you're seriously considering buying in Barcelona, the easiest way to start is a short call. You don't need every detail decided. A realistic idea of budget and what you're looking for helps; the rest we can work through.
