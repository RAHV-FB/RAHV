# Design & Build Brief — Your Home Barcelona

You are the senior brand designer, UX designer, conversion strategist, copywriter and front-end developer responsible for designing the first production-quality website for **Your Home Barcelona**.

Do not treat this as a generic real-estate template. The business wins through trust, familiarity with the United States and Barcelona, personal contact, and access to a licensed local real-estate network. The site should feel premium enough for €1m–€5m buyers, but personal and calm rather than ostentatious.

## 1. Business and positioning

**Brand:** Your Home Barcelona

**Primary person:** Steve Howard.

Steve is a U.S. citizen who has lived in Barcelona since approximately 2006. He is bilingual and previously held real-estate licences in California and Florida. Those U.S. licences are no longer active. He has a real-estate background from the United States, including Beverly Hills, and understands how American buyers tend to think about property, service, negotiation and the buying process.

Steve moved to Barcelona after falling in love with a Spanish woman and with the city, built a family and life here, and has now spent roughly twenty years in Barcelona. That story should add warmth and credibility, but the website must remain focused on the buyer and on Barcelona rather than becoming a personal biography site.

Steve is **not to be presented as a currently licensed estate agent in Catalonia**. His role is the trusted first point of contact, guide and connector for international buyers. He works with a licensed local real-estate professional/team that can carry out the regulated real-estate work and manage the transaction. A lead submitted through the site may ultimately be shared with that licensed partner so the buyer can be assisted.

Do not imply Steve personally holds a Spanish/Catalan real-estate licence, represents himself as a Catalan estate agent, or personally performs regulated services if that is not legally accurate. Use wording such as “former licensed real-estate agent in California and Florida”, “local property guidance”, “a trusted local point of contact”, “working with a licensed real-estate network”, etc.

Do not prominently advertise the partner agency as the consumer-facing brand. The brand is Your Home Barcelona. However, do **not** hide information that is legally required in the privacy policy, legal notice, consent flow or contractual disclosures. Create clear placeholders/TODOs for the exact legal identity of the data controller, partner recipient(s), company details and professional registrations to be inserted after legal review.

## 2. Commercial model

There is no separate upfront advisory fee that Steve wants to charge the buyer. His compensation may arise through a referral/commission arrangement connected to a completed real-estate transaction with the licensed partner network.

Do not make aggressive claims such as “completely free”, “zero cost”, “we cost you nothing” or similar unless the final commercial and legal structure has been verified. If mentioning fees at all, use restrained language such as “No separate advisory fee from Your Home Barcelona” and mark the final wording for legal/commercial confirmation.

The website should attract people who have a real intention to purchase, not casual tourism enquiries. At the same time, do not create an intimidating qualification process. Do not request bank statements, employer details, proof of funds or financial documentation on the public first-contact form.

## 3. Target audience

Primary audience:

- Affluent U.S. buyers considering property in Barcelona.
- Particularly relevant markets: California and New England, while still targeting the United States broadly.
- Typical intended purchase budget: **€1m–€3m**.
- Higher-value options should explicitly allow **€3m–€5m** and **€5m+**.
- Buyers may be purchasing a main residence, second home or investment property. Do not over-segment the public site around those motivations.

Secondary audience:

- English-speaking international buyers who value a personal, English-first point of contact in Barcelona.

The site should be written in excellent natural English first. Architecture should allow Spanish and Catalan versions later, but do not create unnecessary multilingual complexity in V1 unless specifically requested.

## 4. Geographic focus

The brand is Barcelona-first.

Primary focus:

- Barcelona city.

Secondary areas, shown as natural extensions rather than equal headline markets:

- Metropolitan Barcelona / desirable residential areas outside the city, including places such as Sant Just Desvern where buyers may find more space, detached homes and gardens.
- Sitges.
- Castelldefels.
- Costa Brava.

The wider Catalonia network may be mentioned discreetly because the licensed partner network can pass opportunities to appropriate local agents. Do not make the site feel like a general Spain-wide property portal.

Do not make unsupported travel-time claims such as “10 minutes from downtown” unless verified for the exact location and context.

## 5. Core value proposition

The key idea is not “we have the most listings”. The key idea is:

**An American/international buyer can have a trusted person in Barcelona who understands both the U.S. perspective and how life and property buying actually work here.**

Steve can help make the process understandable, discuss areas and lifestyle differences, listen to what the buyer actually wants, introduce the appropriate licensed professionals, and remain a familiar point of contact.

Potential capabilities to communicate, with careful legal wording:

- Initial conversation and needs discovery.
- Explaining the practical differences between buying/living in Barcelona and the United States.
- Neighbourhood orientation.
- Helping identify the type of property and area that fits the buyer.
- Coordination with the licensed local property team.
- Remote property review and video viewings through the team when the buyer is abroad.
- Local viewings and follow-up.
- Access to a broader professional network for legal, mortgage, renovation, utilities and settling-in needs, without implying Steve personally provides legal, tax, mortgage or regulated professional advice.

Never invent partner credentials, numbers of transactions, property inventory, off-market access, awards or “exclusive” stock.

## 6. Primary website objective

This is a **lead-generation website**, not a property portal.

The two primary conversion actions are:

1. **Tell us what you’re looking for** — short, high-quality lead form.
2. **Schedule a call** — Calendly or equivalent integration to be connected later.

Do not build fake listings. Do not fabricate asking prices, addresses, bedrooms, availability, property cards or “featured properties”. If a real listing feed is added later, that can be a separate phase.

The site should make a serious buyer feel comfortable providing a phone number and speaking to Steve.

## 7. Lead form

Keep the initial form concise and high-intent.

Recommended fields:

- Name — required.
- Email — required.
- Phone / WhatsApp — required.
- Current city / country — optional or lightweight.
- Property budget — required, with simple choices:
  - €1m–€3m
  - €3m–€5m
  - €5m+
  - Optional “Other / not decided yet” only if conversion testing suggests it is needed.
- Purchase timeframe:
  - 0–3 months
  - 3–6 months
  - 6–12 months
  - 12+ months / exploring
- Area(s) of interest — Barcelona, Metropolitan Barcelona, Sitges/Castelldefels, Costa Brava, Not sure yet.
- Short optional message: “What are you looking for?”
- Required privacy acknowledgement/consent as legally appropriate, never pre-checked.

Do not ask for salary, employment, bank statements, passport information or proof of funds at this stage. Qualification can happen later through the licensed team.

Provide clear success, error and validation states. After submission, direct the user toward scheduling a call.

Do not silently forward personal data to third parties without the required disclosure and legal basis. The privacy policy and form copy must accurately explain the relevant recipients/partners once the final legal structure is confirmed.

## 8. Calendly / call booking

Design the UI for a future scheduling integration.

Prefer one of these implementations:

- A clean “Schedule a call” button opening the booking service in a new tab; or
- A consent-aware/lazy-loaded embed that does not unnecessarily load third-party trackers before the user interacts.

Do not block the first version on having the final booking URL. Use a clear placeholder configuration value.

## 9. Visual direction

The desired feeling is:

- Light.
- Mediterranean.
- Calm.
- Personal.
- International.
- Premium but approachable.
- Sophisticated without looking like a luxury cliché.

Use Barcelona architecture, natural light, warm stone, pale plaster, subtle Mediterranean greens and carefully controlled warm accents.

A possible palette direction:

- Warm off-white / ivory background.
- Dark slate, deep navy or charcoal text.
- Muted olive / eucalyptus accent.
- Restrained terracotta, sand or warm stone secondary accent.

Do not default to black-and-gold “luxury real estate”.

Avoid:

- Heavy black backgrounds.
- Gold gradients.
- Fake marble textures.
- Glassmorphism.
- Oversized rounded cards everywhere.
- Excessive pill UI.
- Generic skyscraper imagery.
- House-roof, key, map-pin or handshake logo clichés.
- Animated counters.
- Carousels for no reason.
- Floating decorative blobs.
- Excessive scroll animation.
- AI-looking stock composites.
- Copy such as “unlock your dream lifestyle”, “white-glove”, “world-class”, “elite living”, “bespoke journey”, “curated excellence” and similar empty luxury language.

The site should look expensive because it is restrained and well composed, not because it constantly says “luxury”.

## 10. Typography and layout

Use an elegant, editorial hierarchy with excellent legibility.

A refined serif display face paired with a clean sans-serif body can work, but avoid fashion-magazine excess. If external fonts are used, self-host them where licensing allows. Prefer a technically simple solution with no unnecessary third-party font requests.

Layout principles:

- Generous but controlled whitespace.
- Strong alignment.
- Large, confident headlines.
- Comfortable body measure.
- Images should feel architectural and purposeful.
- Avoid stacking every section inside a bordered box.
- Use asymmetry sparingly for sophistication, not as a gimmick.
- Mobile must feel designed, not merely collapsed.

## 11. Branding / logo

Create a clean typographic wordmark for **Your Home Barcelona**.

Possible construction:

YOUR HOME
BARCELONA

or a refined single-line treatment.

Avoid literal rooflines, keys, doors, skyline silhouettes and generic real-estate icons. A subtle YHB monogram can be explored for favicon/social use, but the main brand should remain typographic and credible.

Use Steve’s portrait:

## 14. Homepage structure

Design a homepage approximately along this flow. Improve the layout if you have a stronger solution, but preserve the strategy.

### B. Hero

**Your home in Barcelona, with a trusted local point of contact.**

Supporting idea:

For Americans and international buyers looking for exceptional homes in Barcelona and selected areas of Catalonia. Twenty years of life in Barcelona, a U.S. real-estate background, and access to a licensed local property network.

Hero CTA hierarchy:

### C. Immediate trust / proof line

### D. Why this is different

Explain the gap the service solves:

An American buyer may know how a real-estate transaction works in the U.S. but encounter a very different process, terminology, representation model and lifestyle context in Spain. Steve can explain Barcelona in language and expectations that feel familiar, then connect the buyer with the licensed professionals who can execute the search and transaction.

### E. How it works

Use a clean three-step or four-step flow. Example:

1. **Tell me what you’re looking for** — budget, timing, lifestyle and areas.
2. **Understand the market and neighbourhoods** — clarify what different areas realistically offer.
3. **Search and view with the local team** — appropriate licensed professionals handle property search, viewings, negotiations and transaction work; remote video viewing can be coordinated.
4. **Move forward with the right specialists** — legal, financing, renovation and settling-in contacts as needed.

Keep legal boundaries accurate.

### F. Areas

Show one strong Barcelona-first section with secondary cards/links for:

### G. For Americans buying in Barcelona

## 15. Buying in Barcelona page

This should be genuinely useful rather than SEO filler.

Structure around an American/international buyer’s practical questions:

- What is different from the U.S. process?
- What should happen before viewing seriously?
- How does a buyer make an offer?
- What professionals may be involved?
- What can be done remotely?
- What should a buyer know about neighbourhoods, older buildings and community costs?

Keep the tone explanatory, not alarmist.

Use clear disclaimers that content is general information and not legal, tax or financial advice. Do not invent specific tax percentages or regulatory requirements unless they are researched and cited in the content phase.

## 16. Areas page

The purpose is to help an international buyer orient themselves, not to manufacture thin neighbourhood SEO pages.

Start with four broad choices:

- Barcelona city.
- Metropolitan Barcelona.
- Sitges & Castelldefels.
- Costa Brava.

Within Barcelona, later expansion can cover major neighbourhood profiles such as Eixample, Sarrià-Sant Gervasi, Les Corts/Pedralbes, Gràcia, Poblenou and others only once real, useful local copy exists.

Each area should answer buyer-relevant questions such as:

- What kind of housing is common?
- Apartment vs house.
- Outdoor space.
- Urban vs residential feel.
- Access to the city.
- Who tends to prefer the area?
- What should an overseas buyer understand before narrowing the search?

Do not manufacture precise price-per-m² figures without current data.

## 20. SEO strategy for V1

Build clean technical SEO without keyword stuffing.

Primary intent themes:

- buying property in Barcelona as an American
- Barcelona property for American buyers
- luxury property Barcelona
- English-speaking property guidance Barcelona
- buying a home in Barcelona from the U.S.
- Barcelona property search for international buyers

Be cautious with terms such as “buyer’s agent”, “real-estate agent” or regulated professional titles if using them to describe Steve. They may be useful as search-language context in educational copy, but structured data and business claims must reflect the actual legal status.

Recommended pages should each have:

- unique title tag
- natural meta description
- one clear H1
- semantic headings
- useful internal links
- canonical URL
- Open Graph metadata
- real alt text

Use `Organization` and/or an appropriate general `ProfessionalService` schema unless legal review confirms that a `RealEstateAgent` business type accurately describes the operating entity. Do not falsely mark Steve as a licensed Catalan real-estate agent in structured data.

Do not create fake review schema.

## 21. Privacy, GDPR and legal implementation

Treat this as a serious requirement, not footer decoration.

The business will collect lead information and may pass relevant information to a licensed real-estate partner so the enquiry can be handled.

The final legal texts must accurately state, once confirmed:

- identity of the data controller
- contact information
- purpose(s) of processing
- legal basis
- data fields collected
- recipient categories and, where legally required, named recipients
- any processor relationship
- retention period/criteria
- user rights and contact route
- international transfers, if any
- cookie/tracking technologies
- scheduling/form providers

Do not invent these details. Build obvious placeholders/TODOs for legal counsel or the owner to complete.

Do not use a dark pattern to hide the partner relationship. It can remain out of the marketing narrative, but legally required disclosure must be clear where it belongs.

Consent boxes must not be pre-checked.

Avoid loading non-essential tracking before consent. Prefer privacy-friendly analytics or no analytics in the initial build.

If Calendly or another third-party scheduler is embedded, account for its privacy/cookie implications. A click-to-open implementation is acceptable and may be preferable.

## 22. Technical requirements

Build the site as a production-quality responsive website.

Priorities:

- Excellent mobile design.
- Fast page loads.
- Optimized local images.
- Use supplied WebP files where appropriate.
- Correct `srcset` / responsive image sizing if the stack supports it.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold imagery.
- Avoid cumulative layout shift.
- Semantic HTML.
- WCAG AA-level color contrast and keyboard usability.
- Visible focus states.
- Accessible form labels and errors.
- No unnecessary JavaScript.
- No animation framework unless truly justified.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- No remote dependencies that can be locally served without difficulty.
- Proper favicon and social preview image.
- Custom 404 page if this is a multi-page static site.
- Sitemap and robots configuration when deployment path is known.

Aim for a clean Lighthouse result rather than visually expensive but technically heavy effects.

## 25. Final design principle

Every design and copy decision should support this impression:

**“I am considering spending €1m+ on a home in Barcelona. This looks professional and sophisticated, but more importantly, it feels like I can call this person, explain what I need, and have someone local who understands where I’m coming from.”**

That is the product.

Do not ask follow-up design questions unless a missing fact genuinely blocks implementation. Make sensible, restrained decisions, build the strongest first version, and mark unresolved legal/business details as explicit TODOs rather than inventing them.
